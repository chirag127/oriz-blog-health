Most people overspend at the pharmacy and under-use what they actually need. This is a general, honest guide to shopping smart at Apollo Pharmacy in India: the OTC basics worth keeping, which supplements are evidence-based (and which are money down the drain), and concrete ways to pay less.

> **General information, not medical advice.** This does not recommend prescription medicines or what to take for any condition. Read labels, ask the pharmacist, and see a doctor for anything persistent, severe, or if you take regular medication or have a chronic condition.

## 1. OTC & first-aid basics worth keeping at home

Keep a small home kit — general categories, not specific drug advice:

- **Pain/fever relief** (e.g. paracetamol as a category) — read the label for dose; consult a doctor for persistent fever.
- **Antacids** — for occasional acidity; persistent symptoms need a doctor, not repeat self-dosing.
- **ORS (oral rehydration salts)** — the single most useful thing for dehydration/loose motions; cheap and important.
- **Antiseptic + wound care** — antiseptic liquid/wipes, antibiotic ointment, assorted band-aids, gauze, tape.
- **Cold & allergy basics** — general OTC category; ask the pharmacist which suits you.
- **Tools** — a digital thermometer, and a basic first-aid kit (the American Red Cross checklist is a good template: bandages, tape, antiseptic wipes, gloves, gauze, a cold pack).

First aid is *supportive stabilisation*, not definitive treatment (per NHS/St John Ambulance) — for anything serious, seek professional help.

## 2. Supplements — evidence-based, and when they're useless

Apollo (and its own **Apollo Life** label) sells vitamin D, B12, calcium, iron, omega-3, multivitamins, protein, probiotics. The honest framing (NHS / NIH Office of Dietary Supplements):

- **Most people meet nutrient needs through a varied, balanced diet.** Some people need extra — but *supplementing without a deficiency is often useless, and occasionally harmful.*
- **Get tested and consult a doctor before supplementing.** Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) and iron accumulate to toxic levels if overdone.
- Common genuinely-useful-if-deficient ones in India: **vitamin D, B12** (often low), **iron** (if deficiency confirmed). But confirm with a test first — don't guess.
- **Supplements aren't regulated like drugs** — quality and claims vary; some categories (bodybuilding products) have documented risks from hidden ingredients.

**Rule: test first, supplement second. Don't buy a cabinet of pills on a hunch.**

## 3. Saving money at Apollo Pharmacy

- **Ask for the generic equivalent.** For a branded medicine, the same *salt* (active ingredient) is usually available far cheaper as a generic — ask the pharmacist. Apollo's site even has a **"View all salts"** browse feature to look up medicines by composition, not brand.
- **Apollo's own-label products** (Apollo Life, Apollo Products) — private-label wellness, supplements, personal care and devices, sold at markdowns off MRP (commonly ~10–38% on their own pages; promos vary). Often cheaper than national brands for basics like ORS, aloe gel, devices — compare.
- **Apollo Health Credits (loyalty)** — earned on purchases, but **not** on already-discounted or coupon items (so a promo price and credits usually don't stack).
- **Apollo Circle membership** — an elite membership with deals; **confirm at the counter whether its discount applies to offline in-store pharmacy purchases or only the app** — Apollo's public pages don't state this clearly, so ask before you assume.
- **Senior-citizen / chronic-med refill offers** — ask; discounts and refill programs vary by store and time.

## 4. Smart pharmacy habits (that save money + protect you)

- **Always take the bill** — proof, and it keeps pricing honest.
- **Check the expiry date** at the counter, especially on discounted stock.
- **Know your generic-substitution right** — you can ask for a cheaper same-salt generic.
- **Compare with a Jan Aushadhi (PMBJP) store** — government generic outlets are often the *cheapest* for common medicines; worth comparing for regular/chronic prescriptions *(stock varies by store)*.
- **Don't stockpile OTC meds** — buy what you'll use before expiry.

## Bottom line

At Apollo, the money is saved three ways: **ask for generics, compare its own-label vs national brands, and check whether membership/loyalty actually applies to your purchase.** And the health rule underneath all of it: **OTC and supplements are for minor, general use — for anything real, see a doctor, and test before you supplement.**
