Stress is not a character flaw or something to eliminate. It is your body doing
its job — mobilising you to meet a demand. The problem is when it never switches
off: the demand passes but the alarm keeps ringing. Managing stress is mostly
about giving that alarm reliable ways to stand down.

Here is what has decent evidence behind it, roughly in order of how much return
you get for the effort.

## The unglamorous foundation

Three basics do more for stress than any technique:

- **Sleep.** Short or broken sleep raises next-day stress reactivity — small
  things feel bigger. Stress and poor sleep feed each other, so fixing sleep
  often loosens the whole knot.
- **Movement.** Regular physical activity is one of the most consistent
  stress-reducers we have. It does not need to be intense; a brisk daily walk
  counts. The effect is both immediate (a walk resets a bad afternoon) and
  cumulative.
- **Social contact.** Talking to someone you trust reliably lowers the load,
  even when nothing about the problem changes. Isolation amplifies stress;
  connection buffers it.

If you only do three things, do these. The techniques below help most when this
foundation is in place.

## A breathing pattern that works in the moment

When you are spun up, the fastest lever is your breath — specifically, making
the **exhale longer than the inhale**. A long exhale nudges your nervous system
toward "rest and digest" and slows a racing heart.

A simple version: breathe in for a count of four, out for a count of six, for
about two minutes. No app required. It works because the mechanism is
physiological, not mystical — a slow exhale shifts your autonomic balance. Use
it before a hard conversation, in traffic, or when you wake at 3am with a
spinning mind.

## Name it, then shrink it

A lot of stress is vague dread — a pile of "everything." Two low-effort habits
cut it down to size:

1. **Write it down.** Getting the swirl out of your head and onto paper makes it
   finite. A five-minute brain dump most days is enough.
2. **Separate what you can act on from what you can't.** For the first, pick one
   small next step. For the second, the honest move is to set it down, not to
   keep chewing on it. Rumination — replaying the same worry — is the part of
   stress that does the most damage and the least good.

## What to be skeptical of

- **"Just think positive."** Suppressing stress rarely works and can make it
  worse. Acknowledging it and acting is more effective than pretending.
- **Expensive gadgets and supplements.** Most have weak evidence. The free
  levers — sleep, walking, breathing, talking — are the ones that reliably move
  the needle.
- **Treating relaxation as one more task to perfect.** If your stress-management
  routine is stressing you out, it has missed the point. Keep it small and
  forgiving.

## When it's more than everyday stress

Ordinary stress rises with a demand and eases when the demand passes. Watch for
signs that it has tipped into something that needs support:

- It doesn't lift for weeks, regardless of what's happening around you.
- You lose interest in things you used to enjoy, or sleep and appetite change
  markedly.
- It's interfering with work, relationships, or basic functioning.

If that sounds familiar, talking to a doctor or a mental-health professional is
the practical next step — the same way you'd see someone for a pain that won't
settle. If you are ever in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, contact a
local emergency line or crisis service now. This is general information, not
medical advice.
