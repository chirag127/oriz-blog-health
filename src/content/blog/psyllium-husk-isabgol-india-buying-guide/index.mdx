Psyllium husk — *isabgol* — is one of the cheapest, best-studied fibre supplements you can buy in India, and also one of the most over-priced once brands add flavours, capsules, and packaging. This is a plain buyer's guide: what it is, which form is actually worth buying, and how to get it cheaply (including at Apollo Pharmacy).

> **General information, not medical advice.** Talk to a doctor before starting any supplement — especially if you have a gut condition (IBD, strictures, swallowing trouble), are pregnant, or take regular medication. Psyllium can slow the absorption of other drugs, so take it **separately** (a couple of hours apart), and **always with plenty of water** — taken dry it can swell and choke.

## What psyllium husk actually is

Psyllium is a **soluble fibre** from the seed husk of *Plantago ovata*. In water it forms a gel, which is why it's used generally for **digestive regularity** and as a **fibre top-up**. A couple of honest framing points:

- **Whole-diet fibre is the ideal** — vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains. A supplement is a *top-up* for when your diet falls short, not a replacement for eating fibre-rich food.
- It's a **food-category supplement, not a drug** — in India supplements aren't regulated as strictly as medicines, so quality and claims vary by brand.

## The three forms — and which to actually buy

| Form | What it is | Cost per gram of fibre | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plain husk (loose)** | Just the husk, no flavour/additives | **Cheapest by far** | ✅ Best value |
| **Flavoured powder** | Husk + sweeteners/flavour (e.g. orange) | More expensive (you pay for flavour + sugar/sweetener) | Convenience only |
| **Capsules** | Husk packed into capsules | Most expensive per gram | Only for on-the-go / taste-averse |

**The rule: buy plain husk.** Flavoured powders and capsules cost noticeably more per actual gram of fibre — you're paying for taste and packaging, not more fibre.

## Brands available in India

- **Plain husk (the value picks):** Sat-Isabgol, Telephone brand isabgol, Dabur Nature Care (a laxative-positioned isabgol), Baidyanath, and generic/local isabgol — these are the cheapest per gram. *(Exact current prices vary by seller and pack size — check the label; plain loose husk is typically the lowest cost.)*
- **Premium / organic:** **Organic India Psyllium Whole Husk** — certified-organic (USDA-NOP / Non-GMO), listed around **₹190 for 100 g** (MRP ₹260) on the brand's own site; multi-packs (₹380 for 2, ₹569 for 3) offer only negligible per-gram savings, so bulk isn't much cheaper here. You pay a premium for the organic certification.
- **Imported:** **Metamucil** — a psyllium line sold as powders, capsules, "Fiber Thins", and gummies, in multiple sweetener options (sugar-free, no-added-sugar, real-sugar, stevia). Its powder delivers ~2.4 g soluble fibre/serving (capsules ≥1.8 g). It markets digestive, appetite, blood-sugar and cholesterol angles under the standard US "not intended to diagnose/treat/cure" disclaimer — treat those as *marketing*, and note that's US labelling, not an Indian regulatory claim. Generally the priciest option in India.

## Buying smart — the cheapest route

1. **Buy plain loose husk, not flavoured or capsules** — biggest saving, same fibre.
2. **Compare per-100 g / per-gram, not per-pack** — a "cheap" small pack can cost more per gram.
3. **Local/generic isabgol** at a kirana or a regular chemist is often cheaper than premium branded husk — same plant, same fibre.
4. **Check a Jan Aushadhi (PMBJP) store** if one's nearby — government generic stores are often the cheapest for basic staples *(availability of isabgol varies by store — worth a look, not guaranteed)*.
5. **Start low, go slow, drink water** — too much too fast causes bloating/gas; ramp up.

## Bottom line

Psyllium husk is genuinely useful and genuinely cheap **if you buy it plain**. Skip the flavoured tubs and capsules unless you specifically need them; a plain isabgol pack gives you the same soluble fibre for a fraction of the cost. And whatever the brand — **plenty of water, separate from medicines, and check with your doctor first.**
